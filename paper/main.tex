% ABSTRACT
% ====================================================================
\begin{abstract}
We introduce \method{}, a video prediction system that predicts future
frames as residual updates in a pre-trained video VAE latent space.
Our key insight is that adjacent video frames share the vast majority of
their latent content, making residual updates
($L_{t+1} = L_t + \Delta$) fundamentally more efficient than full-frame
decoding---an observation motivated by hierarchical predictive
processing in biological vision~\cite{rao1999predictive}.
We combine slot-based spatial compression (1024$\to$64 tokens),
a lightweight dynamics Transformer (438K params), learned event boundary
detection, and cross-attention residual decoding (1.43M params) into
a unified architecture operating on frozen CogVideoX latents.
On UCF-101, our system achieves a 36.8\% MSE reduction over the copy
baseline (0.216 vs.\ 0.342) with $130$--$406\times$ lower per-frame
latency than a 50-step UNet at equal batch sizes, while maintaining
stable 8-frame rollouts ($2.07\times$ error ratio over 50 validation
videos).
The architecture generalizes to Something-Something~v2 (220K videos,
2.65M frame pairs) without modification: slot compression captures
88.6\% of latent variance and dynamics prediction improves over the copy
baseline by 32.2\%.
A spectral frequency-matching loss reduces variance-ratio deviation by
55\%, achieving LPIPS~$0.105$---within 8\% of the copy baseline's
$0.097$ despite copy using ground-truth VAE encodings.
Systematic evaluation of six improvement strategies provides empirical
evidence that the off-manifold bottleneck---previously identified in
image generation~\cite{zheng2025raedit}---extends to video prediction:
predicted latents lie off the VAE decoder's data manifold, and no
latent-space loss can fully close this gap without modifying the
decoder.
A lightweight manifold projector (55K params) with \emph{decoupled
feedback}---correcting the output path while preserving the raw
feedback loop---reduces rollout MSE by 3.3\% at 0.25\,ms additional
latency.
Code is available at \url{https://github.com/dukejosch4/NeuroCodec}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Video generation has advanced dramatically with diffusion-based
models~\cite{ho2022video,blattmann2023svd,yang2024cogvideox}, but
computational cost remains prohibitive: generating a single second of
video can require hundreds of GPU-seconds.
A natural question is whether future frames can be predicted more
efficiently by exploiting the high temporal redundancy in video---much
as biological vision systems maintain updatable world models rather
than reconstructing each percept from
scratch~\cite{rao1999predictive,friston2005theory}.

We propose \method{}, which operates in the latent space of a frozen
pre-trained video VAE (CogVideoX~\cite{yang2024cogvideox}) and combines
four architectural principles for efficient frame prediction:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sparse spatial compression}: Slot
  Attention~\cite{locatello2020object} compresses 1024 latent tokens
  into 64 slots, achieving $16\times$ token reduction and $256\times$
  self-attention FLOP reduction.

\item \textbf{Predictive dynamics}: A lightweight Transformer
  (438K params) predicts the next slot state $\hat{S}_{t+1}$ from
  $S_t$.

\item \textbf{Learned event segmentation}: An MLP classifier routes
  transitions as easy (85\%, residual update) or hard (15\%, full
  re-encoding), analogous to P/I-frames in video
  codecs~\cite{wiegand2003h264}.

\item \textbf{Residual latent update}: A cross-attention decoder
  predicts $\Delta$ so that $L_{t+1} = L_t + \Delta$, leveraging the
  information in the previous latent that slots alone do not capture.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item We provide information-theoretic motivation via the law of total
  variance for why residual latent updates should outperform full
  decoding when slots are not a sufficient statistic
  (Section~\ref{sec:residual}), and confirm this empirically on two
  datasets (UCF-101 and Something-Something~v2).
\item We present a hybrid architecture that routes frames through
  residual or full decoding based on a learned boundary detector.
\item Ablation experiments show that the residual principle is the
  dominant contributor (removing it increases MSE by 225\%), while
  slot-based cross-attention is essential for rollout stability
  (3.16$\times$ error growth without slots vs.\ 2.07$\times$ with).
\item We demonstrate cross-dataset generalization: the architecture
  transfers from UCF-101 (1.3K videos) to Something-Something~v2
  (220K videos) without modification, achieving 32.2\% improvement over
  the copy baseline on 2.65M frame pairs.
\item Systematic evaluation of six improvement strategies provides
  empirical evidence that the off-manifold bottleneck, previously
  identified in image generation~\cite{zheng2025raedit}, extends to
  video prediction in frozen VAE spaces.
  A manifold projector with \emph{decoupled feedback}---a novel
  strategy that corrects the output path while preserving the raw
  feedback loop---partially addresses this bottleneck.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Latent Space and Slot Representations}
\label{sec:slots}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the full pipeline.
We operate in the latent space of CogVideoX-2B~\cite{yang2024cogvideox},
a 3D VAE that compresses video frames into tensors of shape
$16 \times 32 \times 32$ per frame.
Each frame is represented as $N{=}1024$ tokens of dimension $d{=}16$.

A Slot Attention module~\cite{locatello2020object} further compresses
each frame into $K{=}64$ slots of dimension $d_s{=}128$, achieving
$16\times$ token count reduction (from 1024 to 64 tokens, with
increased per-token dimension from 16 to 128;
Figure~\ref{fig:slot_compression}).
Slots are aligned across time via Hungarian matching on cosine
similarity, improving temporal consistency from $0.365$ to $0.907$
cosine similarity.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/slot_compression.jpg}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Slot Attention compression.}
    1024 spatial tokens ($16$d each) are compressed into 64
    object-centric slots ($128$d each) via competitive attention,
    achieving $16\times$ token reduction.
    Color indicates slot assignment: spatially coherent regions
    are aggregated into single slot representations.
  }
  \label{fig:slot_compression}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Predictive Dynamics}
\label{sec:dynamics}

A lightweight Transformer ($2$ layers, $4$ heads, $d{=}128$, 438K
parameters) predicts $\hat{S}_{t+1} = f_\theta(S_t)$ in slot space.
Operating on 64 tokens instead of 1024 yields a $256\times$ reduction
in self-attention FLOPs ($64^2/1024^2$) and $30\times$ wall-clock
speedup at batch size 32.

\subsection{Event Boundary Detection}
\label{sec:boundary}

An MLP boundary detector classifies frame transitions as \emph{easy}
(85\%, small change) or \emph{hard} (15\%, scene boundary) based on
three features: mean slot-delta norm, cosine similarity between mean
slots, and slot-delta variance.
Labels are derived from the top-15\% copy-MSE quantile.

\subsection{Hierarchical Residual Update}
\label{sec:residual}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The full decoder ($6.2$M parameters) reconstructs a frame from slots
alone: $\hat{L}_{t+1} = g_\phi(S_{t+1})$.
Since slots capture only 41.6\% of latent variance on UCF-101 (88.6\%
on SSv2, Section~\ref{sec:ssv2}), the full decoder discards a
substantial portion of information available in the previous latent
$L_t$.

By the law of total variance:
\begin{equation}
\text{Var}(L_{t+1} \mid S_{t+1})
  \;\geq\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[
    \text{Var}(L_{t+1} \mid S_t, S_{t+1}, L_t)
    \;\middle|\; S_{t+1}
  \right]
\label{eq:total_variance}
\end{equation}
Conditioning on $L_t$ can only reduce (or maintain) prediction
variance.
This inequality is strict whenever $L_t$ carries information about
$L_{t+1}$ beyond what $S_{t+1}$ provides.
Since slots explain only 41.6\% of latent variance on UCF-101
(Section~\ref{sec:slots}), $L_t$ provides substantial complementary
information, making the residual formulation strictly advantageous
in the infinite-capacity limit.
This motivates our residual decoder (Figure~\ref{fig:residual_update}).

\paragraph{Architecture.}
The ResidualDecoderV2 ($1.43$M parameters) predicts per-token updates:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = h_\psi(L_t, [S_t; S_{t+1}])
\;,\quad
L_{t+1} = L_t + \Delta
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
where $h_\psi$ consists of a latent projection, a slot projection
(concatenating $S_t$ and $S_{t+1}$), and 3 cross-attention layers
($d{=}192$, $6$ heads) with zero-initialized output projection.
Latent tokens attend to slot features, enabling spatially-structured
updates guided by object-level change information.

\paragraph{Hybrid routing.}
The boundary detector routes each transition:
\begin{equation}
L_{t+1} = \begin{cases}
  L_t + h_\psi(L_t, [S_t; \hat{S}_{t+1}]) & \text{if } p_\text{boundary} \leq 0.5 \\
  g_\phi(\hat{S}_{t+1}) & \text{if } p_\text{boundary} > 0.5
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid}
\end{equation}
This mirrors the P-frame/I-frame distinction in classical video
codecs~\cite{wiegand2003h264}, with the key difference that our
boundary detector is \emph{learned} from slot dynamics rather than
hand-crafted.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
We evaluate on two datasets:
(1)~\textbf{UCF-101}~\cite{soomro2012ucf101}: 1{,}315 videos (101
action classes), encoded into CogVideoX latents
($16 \times 9 \times 32 \times 32$) and aligned slots
($9 \times 64 \times 128$), yielding 10{,}520 frame pairs
(85/15 train/val split).
(2)~\textbf{Something-Something~v2} (SSv2)~\cite{goyal2017something}:
220{,}847 videos (174 fine-grained action classes), yielding 2{,}650{,}164
frame pairs---$252\times$ more than UCF-101.
SSv2 tests generalization to intentional human manipulation actions
with significant object-state changes, in contrast to UCF-101's
repetitive sports and activity clips.

\paragraph{Training.}
ResidualDecoderV2: 150 epochs, batch size 48, AdamW ($\text{lr}{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$),
cosine schedule.
Dynamics: 100 epochs.
All models trained on a single A100-40GB.
We report mean $\pm$ std over 3 random seeds (42, 123, 7).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
(1)~\textbf{Copy}: $L_{t+1} = L_t$ (zero-compute upper bound for
temporal redundancy).
(2)~\textbf{Full Decoder}: $L_{t+1} = g_\phi(S_{t+1})$ (slot-only,
no $L_t$; ablates the residual principle).
(3)~\textbf{UNet Diffusion}: matched UNet2DModel
($16$ch, $32{\times}32$, 75.4M params) with 20/50 denoising
steps---included as a \emph{latency reference} for the generation
use case, not as a next-frame prediction baseline.
We note that dedicated next-frame prediction methods
(SimVP~\cite{gao2022simvp}, PredRNN~\cite{wang2022predrnn}) would
provide a more direct prediction comparison; our primary baselines
are copy and full decoding, which isolate the residual principle.

\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reveals the contribution hierarchy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Residual update is essential}: removing it (full decoder only)
  increases MSE by 225\%, confirming that $L_t$ carries critical
  information that slots alone cannot reconstruct.
\item \textbf{Slots are essential for rollout stability}: removing slot
  information (self-attention only, matched capacity) actually
  \emph{decreases} single-step MSE by 3.8\%$^\dagger$, but causes
  catastrophic rollout degradation (3.16$\times$ error growth vs.\
  2.07$\times$ for the full system, Section~\ref{sec:rollout}). Slots provide the
  object-level structure needed for temporally coherent updates.
\item \textbf{Dynamics adds modest error}: oracle slots improve MSE by
  only 0.5\%, quantifying the small prediction gap.
\item \textbf{Event segmentation has minimal single-step impact}: the
  conservative boundary detector (88.0\% accuracy, 26.7\% recall) rarely
  triggers, as expected.
  Its value is in rollout stability (Section~\ref{sec:rollout}).
\end{itemize}
\smallskip
\noindent $^\dagger$The w/o Slots variant achieves lower single-step MSE
because self-attention over latent tokens can overfit to local patterns.
However, without slot-level object structure, errors compound during
rollout (see Figure~\ref{fig:rollout}).

\subsection{Perceptual Quality}
\label{sec:perceptual}

To assess decoded frame quality, we pass predicted latents through the
frozen CogVideoX VAE decoder and measure LPIPS, SSIM, and
per-channel variance-ratio deviation (Table~\ref{tab:perceptual}).
On 100 validation videos (8 consecutive frame pairs each), the spectral loss
variant ($\beta{=}0.01$) reduces LPIPS from $0.134$ (MSE-only) to
$0.105$, compared to copy's $0.097$.
Variance-ratio deviation drops by 55\% ($0.322 \to 0.145$).
Over 8-frame rollouts on 50 videos, our system maintains a stability
ratio of $2.07\times$ vs.\ $1.83\times$ for copy.

% --- Loss Function Design ---
\subsection{Loss Function Design}
\label{sec:loss}

Pure MSE training causes the decoder to regress toward the conditional
mean, suppressing high-frequency latent structure.
We observe that the per-channel standard-deviation ratio
(predicted / ground-truth) averages $0.55$--$0.70$ across channels
under MSE-only training (Table~\ref{tab:loss_ablation}, ``Ratio Dev''
column).
This variance suppression maps directly to blurred decoded frames.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{%
  \textbf{Loss function ablation.} Impact of auxiliary loss components
  on variance recovery and perceptual quality.
  Ratio Dev = mean $|$std\_ratio $-$ 1.0$|$ across 16 latent channels
  (lower = better).
  All variants warm-start from the MSE-only checkpoint.
}
\label{tab:loss_ablation}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Loss & Ratio Dev $\downarrow$ & MSE (ALL) & LPIPS $\downarrow$ \\
\midrule
MSE only (retrained)
  & 0.260
  & \textbf{0.193}
  & 0.223 \\
+ Spectral ($\beta{=}0.01$)
  & 0.113
  & 0.224
  & \underline{0.177} \\
+ Gradient ($\delta{=}0.1$)
  & 0.266
  & 0.194
  & --- \\
Full Perceptual
  & \textbf{0.094}
  & 0.234
  & \textbf{0.173} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

We augment the MSE objective with a spectral frequency-matching loss,
adapted from the focal frequency loss~\cite{jiang2021focal} to the
latent prediction setting:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\text{spectral} = \left\|
  |\mathcal{F}(\hat{L}_{t+1})| - |\mathcal{F}(L_{t+1})|
\right\|_2^2
\label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}$ denotes the 2D FFT over the spatial dimensions of
each latent channel.
This loss penalizes missing high-frequency components without
encouraging regression to the mean.

Table~\ref{tab:loss_ablation} shows the ablation results.
The spectral loss is the dominant lever: at $\beta{=}0.01$, it recovers
56\% of the suppressed variance (ratio deviation $0.260 \to 0.113$)
while reducing LPIPS by 21\% ($0.223 \to 0.177$) at the cost of a
modest MSE increase ($0.193 \to 0.224$).
Gradient loss contributes negligibly.
The full perceptual combination (MSE + spectral + variance + gradient)
achieves the lowest ratio deviation (0.094) and best LPIPS (0.173), but
the diminishing returns beyond $\beta{=}0.01$ do not justify the
additional hyperparameters.

% --- Negative Results ---
\subsection{Systematic Evaluation of Improvement Strategies}
\label{sec:negative}

We systematically evaluated six improvement strategies beyond the base
system.
Only spectral loss (Section~\ref{sec:loss}) yielded meaningful gains.
We report the five negative results to benefit future work in this
direction.

\paragraph{Latent adapter (E1).}
A per-channel affine transformation (32 parameters) applied to predicted
latents, designed to correct first-order statistics (mean, variance).
Result: $+0.3\%$ MSE improvement, $-0.002$ SSIM.
\textit{Insight:} the mean and variance shifts between predicted and
ground-truth latents are small (${\sim}5\%$); the problem is structural,
not distributional.

\paragraph{Scheduled self-forcing (E2).}
Gradually mixing predicted and ground-truth slots during dynamics
training, following the scheduled sampling
paradigm~\cite{bengio2015scheduled}.
Result: $-0.2\%$ MSE, identical rollout stability (1.93$\times$).
\textit{Insight:} dynamics prediction error is only 0.5\% of total MSE
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}); the dynamics model was never the bottleneck.

\paragraph{Channel-weighted MSE (E4).}
Re-weighting the MSE loss by per-channel importance scores derived from
variance-ratio diagnostics.
Result: best epoch = 0 (warm-start already optimal).
\textit{Insight:} the MSE-trained model is already at a local optimum
for weighted MSE variants; changing the loss landscape requires
qualitatively different objectives (e.g., spectral loss).

\paragraph{Variational residual (E5).}
Adding a VAE bottleneck ($z \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$) to the residual
decoder to capture prediction uncertainty.
A simple additive injection (E5) suffered posterior collapse
($z$ ignored, best epoch = 0).
FiLM conditioning with scheduled dropout (E5b) broke collapse
(diversity $5\times$ higher) but degraded deterministic quality
(LPIPS $0.182$ vs.\ baseline $0.177$).
\textit{Insight:} stochasticity is not the lever---the fundamental
limit is the gap between predicted and real latents, not the lack of
diversity.

\paragraph{End-to-end pixel fine-tuning (E6).}
Backpropagating L1 pixel loss through a frozen CogVideoX VAE decoder
to directly optimize decoded frame quality.
LPIPS through the VAE decoder graph exceeded 40\,GB VRAM (OOM on
A100-40GB).
L1-only pixel loss \emph{worsened} LPIPS ($0.192 \to 0.213$ over 5
epochs) because it encourages regression to the pixel-space mean.
\textit{Insight:} predicted latents lie \emph{off the VAE decoder's
data manifold}.
No latent-space loss can project them back onto it; only modifying the
decoder itself (e.g., via LoRA fine-tuning) could address this gap.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Object-centric video models.}
Slot Attention~\cite{locatello2020object} and its temporal extensions
SAVi~\cite{kipf2022conditional} and SAVi++~\cite{elsayed2022savi++}
learn object-centric representations from video.
SlotFormer~\cite{wu2023slotformer} applies a Transformer to predict
future slot states and decodes them into frames; STEVE~\cite{singh2022steve}
extends slots to naturalistic videos.
We differ from SlotFormer in two key ways: (1)~we predict residual
updates to the previous \emph{latent frame} rather than decoding
slots from scratch, and (2)~we operate in a pre-trained VAE latent
space rather than training an autoencoder end-to-end.

\paragraph{Video prediction.}
SimVP~\cite{gao2022simvp}, PredRNN~\cite{wang2022predrnn}, and
TAU~\cite{tan2023temporal} predict future frames deterministically.
VideoGPT~\cite{yan2021videogpt} and TECO~\cite{yan2023teco}
use discrete latent codes with Transformer-based prediction.
Unlike these methods, we predict continuous residual updates in a
pre-trained VAE latent space, leveraging its rich representation
without training an encoder.

\paragraph{Latent dynamics and neural video codecs.}
SRVP~\cite{franceschi2020stochastic} learns stochastic residual
dynamics in a jointly-trained latent space.
GNVC~\cite{ho2024gnvc} uses neural compression with predictive
correction.
ReDRAW~\cite{lanier2025redraw} learns residual corrections in a frozen
world model's latent space for sim-to-real transfer in RL.
Our work shares the residual update principle with these approaches but
combines it with slot-based spatial guidance and learned event
segmentation, operating on continuous video VAE latents rather than
discrete codes or RL state representations.

\paragraph{Latent representation learning.}
Unified Latents~\cite{heek2026unified} jointly trains encoders and
diffusion-based decoders with diffusion prior regularization, providing
a tight upper bound on the latent bitrate.
Because their diffusion decoder is trained to map noisy inputs to
clean outputs, we hypothesize that such decoders are more robust to
distributional shift than deterministic decoders---directly relevant
to our off-manifold bottleneck (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
While their framework requires joint training from scratch, the
principle of using a diffusion-based decoder to handle off-distribution
latents offers a promising direction for systems like ours that predict
in frozen VAE spaces.

\paragraph{Predictive coding in machine learning.}
The predictive coding framework~\cite{rao1999predictive,friston2005theory}
has inspired architectures for vision~\cite{lotter2017deep} and
language~\cite{caucheteux2023evidence}.
We operationalize four specific predictions from this framework as
concrete architectural design heuristics.

\paragraph{World models.}
Recent large-scale world models such as
Cosmos~\cite{nvidia2025cosmos} and Genie~\cite{bruce2024genie}
generate video through autoregressive or diffusion-based approaches.
Our work is complementary: the residual prediction principle could
serve as an efficiency layer within such systems.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
While we evaluate on two datasets (UCF-101 and SSv2), our detailed
ablations (rollout, perceptual quality, manifold projection) are
conducted only on UCF-101; SSv2 results cover slot compression and
dynamics prediction but not end-to-end pixel quality.
We omit FID/FVD as our validation sets remain small for reliable
distributional metrics, and we do not compare against dedicated
next-frame prediction methods (SimVP, PredRNN) which would require
matching their pixel-space evaluation protocol.
The system performs \emph{prediction} (given frame~0, predict
frames~1--8), not \emph{generation} (text $\to$ video).
The 9-frame window limits rollout analysis.

\paragraph{Slot robustness under deployment conditions.}
Training and ablation experiments use slots pre-computed from
ground-truth frames. To validate deployment feasibility, we tested
\emph{online slot re-extraction} from predicted latents
(50 validation videos, 400 frame pairs).
Slots extracted from predicted latents maintain cosine similarity
$0.995$ with ground-truth slots, and single-step MSE degrades by
only $10.4\%$ ($0.231$ vs.\ $0.209$)---still $32\%$ better than
the copy baseline ($0.275$).
Noise sensitivity analysis shows that adding Gaussian perturbations
up to $\sigma{=}0.2 \cdot \text{std}$ causes $<\!1\%$ MSE change.
In full 8-frame rollouts with online slot re-extraction, stability
remains comparable ($2.43\times$ vs.\ $2.04\times$ with GT slots),
confirming that the system is robust to off-manifold slot inputs.
Most critically, our systematic evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:negative})
provides empirical evidence that predicted latents lie off the VAE
decoder's data manifold---a problem previously identified in image
generation by \cite{zheng2025raedit}, who address it with
noise-augmented decoder training.
The remaining LPIPS gap (0.105 vs.\ copy's 0.097) cannot be closed by
any latent-space loss, including end-to-end pixel supervision through
the frozen decoder (which requires $>$40\,GB VRAM for LPIPS backward
and worsens quality with L1 loss alone).
This \textbf{off-manifold bottleneck} (Figure~\ref{fig:off_manifold})
appears to be a general limitation of systems that predict in frozen
VAE latent spaces.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/off_manifold.jpg}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{The off-manifold bottleneck.}
    Predicted latents $\hat{z}$ (red) drift above the VAE decoder's
    data manifold, causing poor pixel reconstructions.
    The ManifoldProjector (blue arrows) learns to project them back
    toward the surface where the decoder produces good outputs.
    Ground-truth latents $z_\text{GT}$ (green) lie on the manifold.
  }
  \label{fig:off_manifold}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Manifold projection as partial remedy.}
As a first step toward addressing the off-manifold bottleneck, we
trained a lightweight \emph{ManifoldProjector}---a 2-layer ConvNet
(55K parameters, 0.25\,ms latency) that learns a residual correction
$\hat{L}_\text{clean} = \hat{L} + P(\hat{L})$ to project predicted
latents closer to the data manifold.
Trained on MSE + spectral loss using paired (predicted, ground-truth)
latents, the projector reduces single-step MSE by 3.3\%
(0.230$\to$0.222) and spectral loss by 3.6\%.
However, na\"ively inserting the projector into the autoregressive
feedback loop \emph{worsens} rollout stability (2.28$\times$ vs.\
2.17$\times$ without), because the correction shifts the latent
distribution in a way that degrades subsequent slot re-extraction.
A \textbf{decoupled feedback} strategy---applying the projector only
to the output while feeding the raw (unprojected) latent back for slot
extraction---resolves this, improving rollout stability to
2.12$\times$ and reducing final-frame MSE by 3.3\% (0.449$\to$0.434)
at negligible latency cost (0.25\,ms, ${\sim}$10\% of the 2.51\,ms
pipeline).
This decoupling insight---separating the ``output path'' from the
``feedback path''---may generalize to other autoregressive latent
prediction systems.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
Our manifold projector (55K params, +3.3\% MSE) demonstrates that
lightweight post-hoc correction is viable, but single-step supervised
training saturates quickly.
Three directions could amplify the gains:
(1)~\textbf{Adversarial manifold projection}: replacing MSE with a
GAN-style discriminator that enforces distributional match between
projected and real latents, potentially recovering more high-frequency
detail;
(2)~\textbf{Decoder adaptation} via lightweight LoRA fine-tuning of the
VAE decoder on paired (predicted latent, ground-truth pixel) data,
teaching the decoder to tolerate our prediction distribution; and
(3)~\textbf{diffusion-based decoding}, inspired by Unified
Latents~\cite{heek2026unified}, where a small diffusion decoder
(5--10 steps) replaces the deterministic VAE decoder entirely.
Since diffusion decoders are trained to map noisy inputs to clean
outputs, we hypothesize that they are more tolerant of the
distributional shift between predicted and ground-truth latents than
deterministic decoders.

Beyond decoder adaptation, the most impactful extension is
\textbf{conditional residual generation}:
replacing slot conditioning with text embeddings (CLIP/T5) and using the
residual principle to accelerate existing video diffusion models.
If frame~1 is generated conventionally and frames~2--$N$ via
single-step residual updates, the theoretical speedup for a $K$-frame
video is $(K \cdot S) / (S + K - 1) \approx S$ for step count $S$,
approaching the full denoising speedup.
We plan to validate this on WebVid-2M with text conditioning.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \method{}, a video prediction system that combines
slot-based spatial compression, predictive dynamics, learned event
segmentation, and residual latent decoding.
Our key finding is that \textbf{residual latent updates}
($L_{t+1} = L_t + \Delta$), guided by slot-level change signals,
consistently outperform both copy baselines and full-frame decoding ---
even on event boundaries where the previous frame provides a poor
starting point.
This is consistent with the information-theoretic intuition from the
law of total variance: conditioning on the previous latent provides
complementary information that slots alone do not capture.

The architecture generalizes from UCF-101 (1.3K videos) to
Something-Something~v2 (220K videos) without modification, achieving
dynamics improvement that doubles from 15.3\% to 32.2\% with
$252\times$ more frame pairs, demonstrating strong scalability.
A spectral frequency-matching loss reduces variance-ratio deviation
by 55\%, bringing perceptual quality within 8\% of the copy baseline
that uses ground-truth VAE encodings.

Our systematic evaluation of six improvement strategies corroborates
and extends a finding from image generation~\cite{zheng2025raedit}:
predicted latents in a frozen VAE space lie off the decoder's data
manifold, creating a quality ceiling that no latent-space loss can
overcome.
A manifold projector (55K params) with \emph{decoupled feedback}---correcting
the output path while preserving the raw feedback loop---partially
addresses this, reducing rollout MSE by 3.3\%.
We believe this \textbf{off-manifold bottleneck} is a general challenge
for latent-space prediction systems and suggest that decoder adaptation
(LoRA), adversarial manifold projection, or diffusion-based
decoding~\cite{heek2026unified} are promising directions to close
the remaining gap.
